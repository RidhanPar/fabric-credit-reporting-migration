## Method

For every month, scope and KPI the reconciliation walks from the legacy figure to
the new one in named steps:

```
Group portfolio balance, 2026-08-31

  legacy published                   34,017,641.78
  F1 double counted product                          -3,336,161.01
  F2 hardcoded Romania FX rate                         -614,059.94
  legacy corrected                   30,067,420.82
  residual against gold                       0.00
  D1 convert each account's balance to EUR and...            -0.14
  gold published                     30,067,420.68
  unexplained                                 0.00
```

* A legacy fault's effect is measured by correcting that formula in a copy of the
  workbook and recalculating it. Not by an opinion about what the formula meant.
* A definition change's effect is measured by having gold compute both
  definitions, so the step is exact.
* The residual is what is left. It must be inside tolerance, otherwise the
  reconciliation is incomplete and nothing should be signed.

Tolerances: EUR 0.1 on amounts, which covers the legacy extract's
rounding to 2 decimals per product and bucket; 1e-07 on rates;
exact on counts.

## Legacy faults found

### F1. A personal loan product is counted twice in the portfolio balance

**Classification:** legacy error. The old report was wrong from 2025-03-31.

**How it was found** (round 0 of the peeling trail, the difference as first reported):

* The shape of the difference: differences start in 2025-03-31 and continue, which suggests something that began that month, such as a product launch or a changed extract
* The shape of the difference: differs with no obvious pattern, inspect the formula
* The balance difference is zero for the first six months of the window and then grows, in all three countries at once. Something started in that month. The product catalogue shows Card Debt Consolidation Loan (CC_CONSOL) launched then.

**What the formula did**

The balance formula adds two SUMIFS: personal loans by product line, then cards by the wildcard product code "CC*". CC_CONSOL is a personal loan whose code begins with CC, so it is matched by both terms and its balance is added twice.

**What it should do**

Select cards by product line, the same way loans are selected, so the two terms cannot overlap.

**Knock on effect**

It also moves the group level 30+ and 90+ rates, because the Summary tab weights each country's rate by that country's EUR balance, and those balances are overstated.

**The fix** (column(s) B of the PL, CZ, RO tab(s), 72 cells):

```
before  =SUMIFS(bal,month,$A5,country,$B$1,line,"Personal Loans")+SUMIFS(bal,month,$A5,country,$B$1,product,"CC*")
after   =SUMIFS(bal,month,$A5,country,$B$1,line,"Personal Loans")+SUMIFS(bal,month,$A5,country,$B$1,line,"Credit Cards")
```

**Measured effect** (recalculating the workbook after the fix):

### F2. Romania is converted to EUR at a hardcoded rate

**Classification:** legacy error. The old report was wrong from 2024-09-30.

**How it was found** (round 1 of the peeling trail, after the earlier faults were corrected):

* The shape of the difference: every month differs by a near constant factor, which suggests a fixed factor or rate applied in one place
* The shape of the difference: every month differs in the same direction and in proportion to the figure, with the proportion drifting slowly, which suggests a factor that was correct once and has not been updated
* Only Romania's EUR figures differ, in every month, and the ratio between legacy and new is constant within a month and drifts steadily across the series. A fixed factor in one country. The RON column that displays the Treasury rate is correct, so the rate is not the problem: the conversion is not using it.

**What the formula did**

The Romania tab multiplies by the literal 0.2012 instead of referencing column C. 0.2012 was the RON rate when the tab was built. The displayed rate column is correct and unused, so the error is invisible on the face of the report.

**What it should do**

Multiply by the month's rate from the Treasury table, as the other two tabs do.

**Knock on effect**

It also moves the group level rates, for the same weighting reason as F1, and it moves the group balance even in the months before the consolidation loan launched.

**The fix** (column(s) D, G of the RO tab(s), 48 cells):

```
before  =B5*0.2012
after   =B5*C5
```

### F3. The 30+ days past due rate uses the previous month's arrears

**Classification:** legacy error. The old report was wrong from 2024-09-30.

**How it was found** (round 2 of the peeling trail, after the earlier faults were corrected):

* The shape of the difference: legacy reports zero in the first month of the series, which suggests the formula reaches back one period
* The 30+ rate is reported as 0.00% in the first month of the window, which is not credible for a mature book, and every month's figure matches the previous month's arrears divided by this month's balance. The 90+ rate on the same tab is fine, so it is not the data.

**What the formula did**

The numerator's SUMIFS filters the balance cube on EOMONTH(month, -1), the previous month end, while the denominator uses the current month. The first month has no previous month in the cube, so the numerator is zero.

**What it should do**

Filter the numerator on the same month as the denominator.

**The fix** (column(s) H of the PL, CZ, RO tab(s), 72 cells):

```
before  =SUMIFS(bal,month,EOMONTH($A5,-1),country,$B$1,dpd,">=30")
after   =SUMIFS(bal,month,$A5,country,$B$1,dpd,">=30")
```

**Measured effect** (recalculating the workbook after the fix):

| Scope | KPI | Months affected | Largest single month effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Czech Republic | 30+ DPD rate | 24 | 3.529% |
| Group | 30+ DPD rate | 24 | 3.043% |
| Poland | 30+ DPD rate | 24 | 2.677% |
| Romania | 30+ DPD rate | 24 | 3.065% |

Largest single effect: 3.529% on 30+ DPD rate for Czech Republic in 2024-09-30.

## Definition changes

These are not errors. Both sides are arithmetically right; the definition
changed and was agreed. Each one is quantified by computing both definitions on gold.

| ID | KPI | Change | Kind | Figures affected | Largest effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Portfolio balance (EUR) | convert each account's balance to EUR and sum, instead of converting the country total | rounding | 59 | EUR 0.79 |
| D2 | New originations (EUR) | convert flows at the monthly average rate, instead of the month end rate | definition | 96 | EUR 9,887.99 |
| D3 | New originations (EUR) | convert each account's amount to EUR and sum, instead of converting the country total | rounding | 12 | EUR 0.40 |
| D4 | 30+ DPD rate | weight the group rate by per account EUR balances, instead of converted country totals | rounding | 0 | none above tolerance |
| D5 | 90+ DPD rate | weight the group rate by per account EUR balances, instead of converted country totals | rounding | 0 | none above tolerance |
| D6 | Approval rate | divide by decisioned applications (approved plus declined), instead of all applications received | definition | 96 | 7.973% |
| D7 | Avg balance per customer (EUR) | divide by distinct customers, instead of active accounts | definition | 96 | EUR 164.24 |
| D8 | Avg balance per customer (EUR) | convert each account's balance to EUR and sum, instead of converting the country total | rounding | 0 | none above tolerance |

Three of these have no effect above tolerance. For a rate, converting each account
to EUR and then dividing gives the same answer as dividing the local currency
totals, because the rate cancels. For the average balance per customer, the per
account rounding is a fraction of a cent once it is divided by the customer count.
They are listed anyway so the chain is complete and nothing is quietly skipped.

## New model errors

0 in this run. The residual is what is left when every known
cause is removed, so a fault in the new model shows up here by elimination.

That is how one was found during the build. The first full reconciliation closed
on amounts and failed on rates by about 0.0000005, because Spark caps the scale
when one decimal column is divided by another, which had truncated every rate in
the gold KPI table to 6 decimal places. It was fixed before any figure was
published, and a data quality check now fails if rates ever look truncated again.
The write up is issue 10 in [ISSUES_AND_FIXES.md](ISSUES_AND_FIXES.md).

## Reproduce this

```bash
py -3.11 -m portfolio_migration reconcile
```

`attribution.csv` (one row per difference and cause), `diagnosis.csv`, and this

figure is left unexplained.
